Raíces / Judhur

Personal project  ·  In progress  ·  SolidWorks

Overview

A pendant lamp combining a 3D-printed PETG lattice skeleton with a cast agar bioplastic skin. The canopy uses an 8-pointed Mudéjar star lattice, drawing on the Spanish-Islamic geometric tradition.

Canopy Form

The canopy is a lofted surface through five profile circles tied to a 3-point arc silhouette, giving a near-spherical ovoid: seam ring r = 2.60″ at Z = 2.5″, widest r = 3.17″ at Z = 5.16″, oculus r = 1.25″ at Z = 8.00″.

A deliberate trade-off: a traditional Mudéjar bell needs an inflection point, which a circular arc cannot produce; it would take a spline. I chose to keep the near-spherical shape and keep the project moving, rather than rework the silhouette for precision the lamp doesn't need.

Lattice

Full 8-pointed stars on 16 angular divisions, with a one-division (22.5°) strut offset. Cell aspect ratios stay near-square across all three bands (1.14, 1.21, 1.33), so the same strut construction works throughout: about 96 diagonals, 16 verticals, and 4 hoops, swept with a 0.10–0.12″ circular profile. Estimated 40–50 g of PETG and about 5 hours of printing.

The top band is left untessellated. The stars would compress about three times there, so open spokes run to a plain compression ring at the oculus, which also tensions the agar skin.

Designing for Printing

The skeleton is modeled as one part and split at its widest point. Each half prints with that face on the bed, so the radius only decreases as it rises and no supports are needed.

A Modeling Problem I Solved

The struts are projected from Top Plane sketches onto the dome surface, then mirrored and circularly patterned 16 times. Below the widest point, a projection hits the surface twice. The fix is to split the loft into one face per band and project band by band. If that fails, the fallback is straight 3D-sketch chords, which sink about 0.055″ at midspan, an acceptable deviation for this lamp.

Next Steps

  1. Split the loft faces at the profile circles if needed, then model the band 1 diagonals and star pattern.
  2. Print band 1 as a roughly 40-minute test coupon before modeling bands 2 and 3.
  3. Print the full skeleton.
  4. Cast the agar skin 2–3 mm wet (5 g agar and 3 g glycerin per 250 ml water) and drape it over the skeleton while leathery.
